# Exercise 04 — Public Artifact Set Plan — Reference Solution

> Module: `mod-112-career-positioning-and-the-next-step-on-the-ladder`
> Exercise type: design / worked example
> Exercise brief: [`lessons/mod-112-career-positioning-and-the-next-step-on-the-ladder/exercises/exercise-04-public-artifact-set-plan.md`](https://github.com/ai-workforce-curriculum/ai-enablement-lead-learning/blob/main/lessons/mod-112-career-positioning-and-the-next-step-on-the-ladder/exercises/exercise-04-public-artifact-set-plan.md) (in the learning repo)

## 1. Solution overview

The deliverable is a **12-month public artifact set plan** — the concrete, bounded publishing calendar for the AI Enablement Lead's externally-visible artifacts (Family B from the mod-111 exercise-05 portfolio). The plan names the artifacts, the publication cadence, the production pipeline, the sanitisation posture, and the measurement by which the Lead decides whether a quarter of publishing was useful.

The plan is **not** a content marketing strategy; the Lead is not a creator. It is the operating discipline for sustaining a bounded set of externally-visible artifacts that compound into the credentials portfolio, support the external positioning in exercise-03, and feed the next-step decision in exercise-02. The horizon is 12 months because artifact pipelines are quarterly; the plan is reviewed at each quarterly portfolio review.

Posture checks this solution encodes:

- **Artifacts are bounded by an annual commitment, not open-ended.** A Lead who commits to "publishing more" has not planned; a Lead who commits to "4 long-form pieces, 12 short-form posts, 2 talks, and 1 open artifact in 12 months" has.
- **The set is a small set.** 4 long-form + 12 short-form + 2 talks + 1-2 open artifacts is the sustained cap for a Lead with a day job. Exceeding the cap erodes day-job performance, which is the dominant signal; falling well below the cap is stall-shaped and reads as lapsed externally.
- **Every artifact is sanitised before publication.** The two-part confidentiality test from mod-111 exercise-05 is the publication gate. Specifics that fail either part are rewritten or held.
- **Every artifact ladders to the next-step ambition.** Artifacts that do not advance the ambition selected in exercise-02 are nice but off-strategy; the plan surfaces them and the Lead chooses honestly.
- **The production pipeline has four stages and is dated, not vibes-driven.** Idea, draft, review, publish — each stage has a concrete lead time and a named reviewer where required.
- **Co-authorship and attribution are explicit.** Where an artifact draws on collective CoE work, the plan names the collaborators and the consent conversation up-front, not after a draft is written.
- **The plan has a stop-the-line condition.** Live incidents, unresolved audit findings, open regulator interactions, and material organisation-level events pause publishing. Publishing through a crisis is a career-level mistake.
- **The measurement for "was this useful?" is outcome-level, not vanity-metric.** Downloads and likes are read but are not the discriminator; signalled next-step opportunities, invited talks, peer-cited artifacts, and reference-caller mentions are.

## 2. Implementation — worked answer (`public-artifact-set-plan.md`)

### 2.1 Artifact types and their roles

Four artifact types. Every item in the plan sits in exactly one type.

| Type | Role | Reach | Half-life | Typical production time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1. Long-form piece** | Primary craft-demonstration artifact; the artifact R2 (hiring manager) and R3 (hiring-committee peer) read for depth | 500-5,000 readers per piece at mid-career authority | 12-36 months (evergreen if framework-anchored) | 15-25 hours per piece across 3-4 weeks |
| **T2. Short-form post** | Weekly-to-monthly presence signal; keeps the external activity feed fresh; reposts long-form or picks up a current field debate | 100-2,000 impressions per post | 1-4 weeks | 1-3 hours per post |
| **T3. Talk / conference appearance** | High-signal craft demonstration; also builds C (community) via program-committee relationships | 50-500 in-room; 500-5,000 on recording | 12-24 months | 20-30 hours per talk across 6-8 weeks |
| **T4. Open artifact** | Template, rubric, worked example published under a licence; the artifact that peer practitioners cite | Depends on topic; strongest for templates practitioners adopt | 24-48 months (longest half-life) | 25-40 hours per artifact across 4-8 weeks |

### 2.2 Annual volume targets

For a mid-career Lead with a day job, running a 12-month plan that respects the mod-111 exercise-05 ≤ 10% of working time ceiling:

| Type | Annual target | Quarterly target | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **T1. Long-form** | 4 | 1 per quarter | ~70-100 hours per year |
| **T2. Short-form** | 12-18 | 3-5 per quarter | ~24-45 hours per year |
| **T3. Talks** | 2 | 1 per half | ~40-60 hours per year |
| **T4. Open artifacts** | 1-2 | at Q2 and Q4 | ~25-80 hours per year |
| **Total** | 19-26 artifacts | | ~160-285 hours per year (~6-10% of working time) |

Volume above this cap is the fail-state. A Lead publishing 8 long-form pieces in a year is a Lead whose day-job scorecard is suffering, and the day-job scorecard is the dominant signal. Volume well below (say, 1 long-form and no talks) is also a fail-state: stall-shaped; reads as lapsed in a cold-read drill (exercise-03 §5.1).

### 2.4 Topic sourcing — where artifacts come from

Topics for the twelve months are **not** invented each month. They are sourced from six predictable buckets:

| Bucket | What it is | Example (from the worked calendar) |
|---|---|---|
| **B1. Sanitised operating artifacts** | Templates, rubrics, and worked examples from the Lead's own operating work, sanitised per mod-111 ex-05 | A1 (board review template), A9 (commissioning-brief template), A19 (ritual plan template) |
| **B2. Debate-shapers in the field** | Current practitioner conversations the Lead has a specific view on | A5 (attribution-honesty in ROI claims), A10 (incident response in enablement) |
| **B3. Retrospectives** | What the Lead learned over a cycle, with specific surprises named | A14 (12-month retrospective) |
| **B4. Framework translations** | Translating a framework (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, OWASP ML Top 10, MITRE ATLAS) into operator-facing guidance | Opening framing of A1; A5 ROI-honesty posture |
| **B5. Reactions to published events** | Posts that respond to a published industry event, regulator action, or well-known incident at another organisation | Occasional A3, A11, A13 shapes |
| **B6. Panels and talks** | Talk accept-dates generate adjacent short-form and long-form | A6 → A7, A8; A15 → A16, A17 |

A plan where all 19-26 artifacts come from B5 (reactions) alone is a content-chase. A plan with a balance across all six buckets is a plan that compounds.

### 2.5 Production pipeline — four stages, dated

Every artifact moves through the same four stages with concrete lead times. The pipeline is the discipline that converts "I'd like to publish more" into shipped artifacts.

| Stage | What happens | Lead time (T1 long-form) | Lead time (T4 open artifact) | Who's involved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1. Idea** | Topic selected; bucket assigned; working title; intended venue | 1 week before S2 | 1-2 weeks before S2 | Lead alone |
| **S2. Draft** | First draft; sanitisation applied; the two-part confidentiality test run | 2-3 weeks | 3-4 weeks | Lead alone; co-authors if applicable |
| **S3. Review** | Mentor / peer review (craft feedback); exec sponsor review *only if* the content approaches the confidentiality line and borderline | 2 weeks | 2-3 weeks | 1-2 peer reviewers; exec sponsor on borderline items only |
| **S4. Publish** | Final edit; venue submission or direct publish; linked from canonical personal site; LinkedIn cross-post | 1 week | 1-2 weeks | Lead alone |

For T2 (short-form), the pipeline collapses to S1/S2/S4 (one week each); S3 is skipped unless the post is borderline on confidentiality.

For T3 (talk), the pipeline is: CFP response (S1), acceptance (S1-S2 intermediate), prep and rehearsal (S2 and S3 merged), deliver (S4). CFP-to-deliver is 6-9 months for the better venues; CFP-to-reject is 8-12 weeks if the submission doesn't land.

### 2.6 Sanitisation posture — the publication gate

Every artifact passes through sanitisation before S3 review. The two-part test from mod-111 exercise-05 §2.5.3:

1. **Could this piece be published if my employer's name were attached to the byline?** If yes, piece proceeds. If no, piece returns to S2 for further sanitisation.
2. **Would I be comfortable if the piece were read by my exec sponsor before publication?** If yes, piece proceeds. If no, piece returns to S2.

Specific items that routinely fail and must be sanitised:

- **Named vendors** → category-level vendor language only.
- **Named tools in-use** → "a widely-adopted AI coding assistant" / "a major AI chat-assistant platform."
- **Scorecard numbers** → publicly disclosed only; pattern-level otherwise ("retired multiple vendors in one cycle").
- **Named incidents at current org** → never; discuss patterns of incidents, not specifics.
- **Named colleagues without consent** → removed or anonymised; consent recorded in writing if named.
- **Governance-council deliberations** → never published; patterns of governance discussion are safe.
- **Content from the champion CoP field-reports channel** → never published.
- **Exec-sponsor conversations** → never published.
- **Specific named clients / customers / regulators** → never published.

Items that fail sanitisation can often be reframed as pattern-level artifacts. A piece about "the mistake we made in commissioning <internal tool>" fails; a piece about "three failure modes we observed in internal-tool commissioning and how we would commission differently today" with specifics scrubbed to patterns passes.

### 2.7 Co-authorship and attribution

For any artifact that draws on collective CoE work (the majority of long-form and all open artifacts), co-authorship is handled up-front:

- **Before S1,** the Lead names the collaborators whose work underlies the artifact. If attribution is unclear, the artifact is reframed to pattern-level.
- **At S1 or early S2,** the Lead has a consent conversation with named collaborators. Co-authorship is offered where the collaborator's contribution is substantial; acknowledgment is offered where contribution is specific but not co-authorship-level.
- **In S2 draft,** co-authors are named in the byline; acknowledgments are named in the artifact's closing.
- **At S3 review,** co-authors read the draft; their feedback is binding on their own contributions.
- **At S4 publish,** the artifact's attribution is identical to the final draft; no attribution change after publish without co-author consent.

A Lead who publishes a piece heavily drawing on collective work under their sole byline without co-authorship is a Lead whose next reference-check will not go well. Honest attribution compounds; inflated attribution is self-correcting in a career-damaging way.

### 2.8 Stop-the-line conditions

The plan is paused — no new publication, no new CFP responses — when any of:

- **A live incident (mod-109)** is in progress at the current organisation, until the incident is closed and the Lead has written the internal post-mortem.
- **An open high-severity audit finding or regulatory interaction** is unresolved, until it is closed.
- **A material organisation-level event** (acquisition, divestiture, leadership change at the sponsor level) that changes what is safely discussable externally.
- **The Lead's day-job scorecard shows regression** on a non-regression floor (mod-106) for two consecutive quarters and the Lead has not yet diagnosed the cause.
- **The Lead is actively interviewing for an O3 role** (exercise-02 vertical out-of-org) — new artifacts pause until the process resolves one way or the other, to avoid the publication feeding an interview the current employer does not yet know about.

Stop-the-line resumes when the condition closes. Pre-committed obligations (an accepted talk, a submitted article) are honoured where possible; new commitments are deferred.

### 2.9 Measurement — was this quarter of publishing useful?

The plan is measured quarterly, in the same spirit as the CoE's quarterly operating rituals. Measurement is outcome-level, not vanity-metric.

| Measure | What counts | Signal strength |
|---|---|---|
| **M1. Invited talks** | Unsolicited speaking invitations in the quarter | Strong — the field is naming the Lead |
| **M2. Peer-cited artifacts** | Other practitioners publicly cite or link one of the Lead's artifacts | Strong — long half-life compounding |
| **M3. Reference-caller mentions** | References have been called about the Lead and mentioned the external profile as a reason for the call | Strong — shows positioning is landing with hiring managers |
| **M4. Sponsor commentary** | The exec sponsor has mentioned the Lead's external profile in a 1:1 (positively, neutrally, or concerning — any mention is a signal) | Medium — tells the Lead what the sponsor sees |
| **M5. Recruiter inbound quality** | Fraction of recruiter inbound that is specific-context (named role, named thesis for why the Lead fits) vs. generic blast | Medium — tells the Lead whether the positioning filters correctly |
| **M6. Short-form engagement (quality)** | Comments and reposts from named peers (not strangers) on the short-form feed | Medium — community-contribution evidence |
| **M7. Vanity metrics** | Likes, impressions, follower count | Weak — read but not the discriminator; chased only on the margin |

The quarterly review names what worked and what did not. Patterns across 2-3 quarters (not single quarters) drive plan adjustments: a long-form topic that produced an M1 invited talk is a topic the Lead publishes more on; a long-form that produced no M1 / M2 / M3 after two quarters of promotion is a topic the Lead stops pursuing.

### 2.10 Review cadence

- **Weekly:** Lead checks the publishing calendar for the upcoming artifact's next stage transition. 15 minutes.
- **Monthly:** Lead reviews short-form engagement at the M6 level, triages the month's opportunities, declines or accepts CFPs in flight. 45 minutes.
- **Quarterly:** Lead runs the full quarterly review — measurements M1-M7, plan vs. actuals, next-quarter calendar locked, stop-the-line check. 90 minutes, aligned with the mod-111 exercise-05 quarterly portfolio review.
- **Annually:** Lead reviews the 12-month plan against the next-step decision from exercise-02; recalibrates topic mix, venue mix, and volume targets for the next 12 months.

### 2.11 Decision rationale — why this shape, not another

- **Why a 12-month plan, not an open-ended publishing commitment.** Publishing without a horizon drifts; publishing with a 12-month horizon converts intention into dated commitments. The horizon matches the next-step decision's natural cadence (exercise-02 is quarterly-reviewed; the public artifact plan is annually-reviewed).
- **Why the volume cap (~20-25 artifacts/year).** Below this, the external profile reads as lapsed in a cold-read drill (exercise-03 §5.1). Above this, the day-job scorecard starts to slip, and the day-job scorecard is the dominant signal. The cap is the control.
- **Why four artifact types, not six or eight.** Four types cover the externally-visible craft-signalling surface; adding more (newsletters, podcasts, YouTube) creates a content-marketing stack a day-job operator cannot sustain.
- **Why 1 long-form per quarter, 4 per year.** One long-form per quarter is the sustainable rhythm for a mid-career Lead; it matches the natural "I have a thought worth 20 hours" cadence most operators run at. Two per quarter burns out by month 4; one per half-year reads as stalled.
- **Why topics come from six predictable buckets.** Invented topics are expensive and risk either generic-ness or confidentiality drift. Topics sourced from the Lead's actual operating work (B1), the field's actual debates (B2), the Lead's actual retrospectives (B3), framework translations (B4), reactions to published events (B5), and talks (B6) are cheap to source and honest.
- **Why the pipeline has four dated stages.** Artifacts without dated stages drift; the pipeline is the operating discipline. Lead times (3-4 weeks for long-form idea→publish) match typical mid-career operator writing velocity.
- **Why co-authorship is handled up-front, not after a draft lands.** Retroactive co-authorship conversations read as transactional; up-front ones read as honest. Honest attribution compounds the Lead's credibility over years; retroactive attribution often costs more than the artifact contributes.
- **Why stop-the-line exists.** Publishing through a live incident, an open audit finding, or a sponsor change is net-negative: the Lead is read as inattentive at best, careerist at worst. The stop-the-line condition is cheap insurance against a low-probability, career-damaging mistake.
- **Why measurement weights M1-M3 strong and M7 weak.** Invited talks (M1), peer citations (M2), and reference-caller mentions (M3) directly advance the next-step ambition. Impressions and likes (M7) do not; chasing them produces the content-marketing stack the plan explicitly avoids.
- **Why review cadence is weekly / monthly / quarterly / annual.** Weekly check keeps the pipeline unblocked; monthly review triages opportunities; quarterly review measures outcomes; annual review recalibrates against the exercise-02 ambition.
- **Why Q2 is the heavy quarter and Q4 closes the year with retrospective + second talk.** The year's cadence has to match natural production rhythms: Q1 opens with the template-shaped long-form (B1) that is easiest to produce; Q2 lands the conference talk whose CFP went out the prior Q4; Q3 publishes the incident-response differentiator; Q4 closes with the retrospective (B3) and the second talk at a bigger venue. Shifting these would create either production clustering or publishing stalls.

## 3. Validation steps

1. **Four artifact types named** (long-form, short-form, talk, open artifact) with role, reach, half-life, production time.
2. **Annual volume targets** named per type with explicit time-cost totals under the 10% working-time ceiling.
3. **12-month calendar** with specific artifacts named per quarter, type-tagged, topic at pattern level, venue named, key dates set.
4. **Topic sourcing from ≥ 6 buckets** named; the calendar's topics trace back to at least four of the six buckets.
5. **Production pipeline** has four dated stages (idea, draft, review, publish) with per-type lead times.
6. **Sanitisation posture** — the two-part confidentiality test is the publication gate; a named list of items that routinely fail sanitisation is provided.
7. **Co-authorship and attribution** handled up-front with a documented consent conversation at S1/S2.
8. **Stop-the-line conditions** named with ≥ 4 conditions and a resume protocol.
9. **Measurement M1-M7** named with signal strength per measure; vanity metrics tagged weak.
10. **Review cadence** names weekly / monthly / quarterly / annual rhythms.
11. **Plan calibrated to exercise-02 next-step ambition** explicitly.
12. **`org-specific` / `author-to-confirm` tags** on all organisation-dependent specifics (dates, venues, named external publications the Lead has access to).

## 5. Common mistakes

- **Open-ended publishing commitment.** "I want to publish more" is not a plan. Dated artifacts in a 12-month calendar is a plan.
- **Volume above the 10% ceiling.** 8 long-form pieces a year erodes the day-job scorecard. The ceiling is the control; exceeding it is a trade-off against the dominant signal.
- **Volume well below the floor.** A single artifact in 12 months reads as lapsed in a cold-read drill. If the plan can't sustain the floor, reduce scope — fewer long-form, more short-form — rather than skipping the floor entirely.
- **All T2 (short-form) and no T1 or T4.** Short-form keeps the activity feed fresh but does not demonstrate operating altitude; T1 (long-form) and T4 (open artifacts) are what R2 and R3 read for depth.
- **Topics invented monthly.** The content-marketing failure mode. Topics sourced from the six buckets (B1-B6) are cheap and honest; invented topics are expensive and often confidentiality-adjacent.
- **No dated pipeline.** "I'll get to it" is the default state of an undated pipeline. Dated S1-S4 with lead times is the discipline.
- **Sanitisation applied at S4 (publish) instead of S2 (draft).** Sanitising late means re-drafting; sanitising at S2 saves rework.
- **Specifics that fail the two-part test.** Named vendors, scorecard numbers, specific incidents, named colleagues without consent, governance-council deliberations — any of these makes the artifact a career-damaging mistake.
- **Retroactive co-authorship.** Adding co-authors after a draft lands reads as transactional. Up-front conversation at S1/S2 is the norm.
- **Publishing through a live incident.** The Lead who publishes a governance-posture piece while an open high-severity finding is unresolved is the Lead whose reference check surfaces the incident and the article in the same paragraph. Stop-the-line is cheap insurance.
- **Publishing through an active interview process.** An unknown-to-sponsor interview that gets fed by a new long-form is a Lead whose departure is both discovered and read as careerist simultaneously. Pause.
- **Chasing vanity metrics.** Likes and impressions optimise for a content-marketing operator, not a Head-of candidate. M1 (invited talks), M2 (peer citations), M3 (reference-caller mentions) are the discriminators.
- **No stop-the-line condition.** A plan without stop-the-line is a plan assuming the next 12 months are boringly calm. Most 12-month windows are not.
- **Co-authored work claimed as solo.** The single most reliable reference-check failure in mid-career AI enablement hires. Honest attribution compounds; inflated attribution is self-correcting.
- **Plan calibrated to no specific ambition.** A plan that publishes across all possible next-step ambitions is a plan that pattern-matches to none. Calibrate to one or two.
- **Annual review that just extends the prior year by 12 months.** Annual review recalibrates against exercise-02's current ambition; if the ambition has shifted, the plan's topic mix, venue mix, and open-artifact bets shift too.

### 5.1 Stretch — the compounding year

A single 12-month plan is a bounded commitment. The compounding happens over 3-4 years. By year 2, the Lead has a body of 6-8 long-form pieces, 24-36 short-form posts, 4 talks, and 3-4 open artifacts — a Family B portfolio with enough substance that R2 hiring managers read it as a Head-of candidate without needing the resume's inline context.

The compounding is not linear: the Q2 open artifact (A9 in the worked calendar) in year 1 produces most of its reach in years 2 and 3 as practitioners adopt and cite it. The second-year calendar ships artifacts that reference the first-year's open artifact; the third-year calendar ships artifacts that build on the citations pattern. A Lead who runs this plan for one year will not see the full effect; a Lead who runs it for three years will.

The discipline is not "publish for one year to get the next role." It is "operate a bounded publishing cadence sustainably for three years, so that when the next role becomes available, the portfolio reads at Head-of altitude on cold-read." A Lead running the plan with a single-year outlook burns out around month 7; a Lead running it with a three-year outlook survives the natural low-productivity quarters by leaning on short-form and talks and resumes long-form the next quarter.
